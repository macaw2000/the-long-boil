# The first day: what a new player learns, and when

Written 3 November 2026, after a long playtest chat with relatives who had never played an idle game. It replaces GDD section 7's "first session (about 20 minutes)" as the target for a new player's first day. GDD section 7 should be amended to match once Jeremy agrees.

## What the playtesters said

- "Your first 20 minutes plan is too much... I remember seeing or encountering pretty much everything in that list. But I didn't hang onto any of it. Perhaps... make it over the first 24 hours. And expand upon each item in turn til we learn the world."
- "Your story box takes us immediately into interactions with the world outside of the tinker's shop, and we aren't quite ready for other characters and problems yet because we aren't grounded in the basics enough yet. I think that's why I had to read each of them about three times... all while I'm still trying to figure out what the mechanics of the game are."
- "I do need a reason to care about building wrenches and fighting rats." The early scenes "must either be high stakes choices (danger to the character to make them invested in the tinker as a person), or... something that allows the player to create something of their choice so they feel like your game is 'their' world." They "also all need to teach us who the tinker is, how we got here, why on earth rats are the enemy."
- "List out what your player needs to learn in order for mechanics and in a column next to it put the story points in order and don't make the player do too many steps on one screen. Plan out their learning progression."
- Earlier, from the same family: too many names too fast (work order 036), no hook (work order 035), and terms like Crowns, the Counting-House and "spike the bill" unexplained.

## What the first sitting asks of a player today

Measured by the Act I walk (work order 036): the first sitting runs about 29 minutes and ends with every Prologue scene played on exactly one full bar of Nerve. In that time a player meets:

- **Mechanics (12):** the Boiler and steam, queueing a job, waiting on a job, the Story tab and letters, Nerve, equipping, fighting, Rations, Crowns, selling at the Counting-House, paying a debt, Arrears. The guide then adds armour (Tailoring or Smithing) and the Loadout, and the scenes add challenges with odds.
- **Story:** ten scenes in six places (the workshop, the Row, the Polytechnic, Rustmoor's gate, a rat nest, a lecture hall), six named people or groups, and a dozen glossed terms.
- **And what it never says:** who you are, or why Hester's workshop came to you.

That is roughly one new thing a minute. Each piece is now explained (work orders 035 and 036), but explained is not learned: a player who is still finding the Start button cannot also hold a city's history. The fix is not better sentences. It is fewer things at once, in an order where each one is needed the moment it is taught.

## The rules this plan follows

1. **One new thing per screen.** A guide step teaches one control. A story scene introduces at most one new person, place or term, and only one that the scene needs.
2. **Teach a mechanic when the story needs it, and give a story reason for every mechanic.** You fight rats because they are in your workshop taking your spanners, not because the guide says so.
3. **The first sitting stays at No. 9.** Its only step outside is the first fight, which follows the rats down through the floor. The street, the city and its institutions wait until the player can work the bench without thinking.
4. **The first scene is about you.** Who you are, why you are here, and one choice that is yours.
5. **Something is at stake early.** A cold Boiler that may not take the pressure; rats in the workshop at night. Danger to the Tinker, not to the city.
6. **Stories fill the waits.** A scene opens while a job runs, so reading never stops the work and the work never makes the reader wait. (The first playtester liked this most.)
7. **Leave a question at the end of every sitting,** answered at the start of the next.
8. **Spread it over the first day.** Three sittings: the first about 10 minutes, then two more a few hours apart. A sitting is a counted check-in (`record.check_ins`, an hour or more after the last).

## The progression

"You learn" is the one mechanic the step teaches. "You do" is the one thing the screen asks. "New in the story" is the one person, place or term the scene introduces. "You can now say" is what a player should be able to tell someone afterwards; the educator checks these.

Revised 3 November 2026 after the educator's baseline (the Ledger below): the binding moves out of the first scene, Rustmoor is named once, the bill no longer teaches four terms, odds wait for the register, the lecture keeps only what the plate needs, and story and work alternate in every sitting.

## What it changes

**Content (the scrivener):**
- Two new first scenes for sitting 1: who you are, with the three-way choice and a new flag for it; and rats in the workshop.
- The bill moves to sitting 2, with both options certain. The binding moves to the receipt. The neighbours and the missing tins become one scene. The tip book and the nest stay; the lecture loses the Great Kettle and the Lift; the Gazette folds into the brass plate.
- Prologue scenes are gated by `record.check_ins` so each sitting opens only its own, and ten scenes still fit the day's Nerve.
- The register gets a skill-check option, to teach odds.
- The guide's lines are rewritten for the new steps; Brassbuckle's vouching line goes with the first sale.

**Client (the lead session):**
- The guide (`Guide.tsx`) runs sitting 1 only; sitting 2's and 3's steps are goal lines. The "Play a scene under Open to you" step goes.
- Each Needle stays hidden until a scene first moves it.
- When the next sitting's scenes are not open yet, Story shows a calm line and when ("More from the Row when you are next back, after 14:30"), never a list of requirements.
- The debt line points to where the bill is paid only once that place is open.
- Armour shows what it protects.
- The sign-in letter says the city flies.

**Design:** GDD section 7's "The first session (about 20 minutes)" becomes "The first day (three sittings)"; canon records the three origins.

## Decisions for Jeremy

1. **Why did No. 9 come to you?** Canon does not say (characters.md lists "what Pip calls the player" as open). The playtester's best idea was a choice that makes the world yours. One scene could ask it: Hester's apprentice once; a distant relative named in her will; a stranger whose name she put down for reasons nobody knows. Each sets a flag that later lines can colour. Decided (Jeremy, 3 November 2026): the player chooses, in the first scene, between Hester's apprentice, a relative named in her will, and a stranger.
2. **Looks.** Changing the Tinker's hair or outfit is a real hook in other games, but every pose and every weapon is drawn on one Tinker (four Gemini sheets today, with more as weapons are added), so each new look means redrawing all of them. Recommend: not now; revisit with Cog-bought looks (GDD section 10).
3. **Three sittings in the first day**, with the brass plate at the end of the third. Agreed (Jeremy, 3 November 2026).
4. **Selling before the register.** Brassbuckle vouches for you at the first sale until you sign (Jeremy, 3 November 2026).
5. **Needles.** Each stays hidden until a scene first moves it (Jeremy, 3 November 2026).
6. **Sitting 3's pace.** One action between each pair of scenes, asked by the goal line; scenes are not tied to jobs finishing (Jeremy, 3 November 2026).
7. **Origin in later scenes.** Neutral wording: every scene must read true for an apprentice, a relative and a stranger; an origin may add an option of its own (an option can require its flag), but scene text does not vary (Jeremy, 3 November 2026, after the educator's first check of draft 037).
8. **The bill's stakes.** The draftsman and assayer resize Pip's bill and the Rust Rat Runt's Crowns together, so one night of fighting does not cover the bill and selling matters (Jeremy, 3 November 2026).
9. **The first armour** does visibly more; a tuning question for the draftsman (Jeremy, 3 November 2026).
10. **The register's first odds** are at par, difficulty 2 for a Tinkering 2 player (about 60%), and its Polytechnic Favour stays behind that check (Jeremy, 3 November 2026).
11. **The Prologue's item changes** in draft 037 (Rations, Rat Scrap) are accepted without a draftsman review (Jeremy, 3 November 2026).
12. **The bill and the economy.** Pip's bill is 1,400 Crowns, a year's rent at 350 a quarter; the repair takes 80 off for 10 Rat Scrap; a Rust Rat Runt drops 1 to 2 Crowns; Rat Scrap sells for 8. Pip's receipt scene does not wait for payment: Pip calls either way, and clearing the bill is a goal across the second and third sittings (Jeremy, 3 November 2026).
13. **Armour counts four times as much** (ARMOUR_HALF_POINT 25, every monster's hit rescaled to keep the reference player's damage), with the draftsman to restate the tables it leaves stale and settle what a Charm is worth (Jeremy, 3 November 2026).
14. **Start now keeps the queue.** It puts the new job first and the rest waits behind it, as far as the queue has room; a full queue loses its last job, and the screen says which first. The armour goal comes after the sitting's open scenes (Jeremy, 4 November 2026, after the third check: "making it easy to learn and play").
15. **Ledger Page 1 waits until after the plate**, so sitting 3 does not also carry a page drop (taken on the lead session's recommendation, Jeremy, 4 November 2026).
16. **Hester's Ledger stays hidden until the receipt finds the binding** (taken on the lead session's recommendation, Jeremy, 4 November 2026).
17. **The goal bar names the next skill gate** once Act I opens, such as "Train Scavenging to 9": after sitting 3 nothing else told a player to train, and a player who never acts on Story's line reaches rook_at_the_tip about day 25 to 34 instead of day 7 to 9 (Jeremy, 4 November 2026, after the assayer's walk).
18. **The walk is fixed**: its alternation counter, Scald handling and failed-pass handling, with 21 days as the default horizon (Jeremy, 4 November 2026).
19. **An evening starter clearing the bill at sitting 2 is fine**: an early win that still needs the sale; decision 12 stands (Jeremy, 4 November 2026).
20. **Three invented facts stay**: Hester has been gone a year; on the Friday before she went she gave Pip a toffee and promised the rent next week; four names on the tip book's page never came back (Jeremy, 4 November 2026).

## Measuring it

The educator (`.claude/agents/educator.md`) checks a draft of any of this against the "You can now say" column: it walks the first day in order, counts what each screen asks of a player, and runs cold reads with a reader who has never played an idle game. It tells the scrivener what was missed, confused, or dull, and the scrivener rewrites until the reader can say each line.
