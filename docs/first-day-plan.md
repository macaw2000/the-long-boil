# The first day: what a new player learns, and when

Written 3 November 2026, after a long playtest chat with relatives who had never played an idle game. It replaces GDD section 7's "first session (about 20 minutes)" as the target for a new player's first day. GDD section 7 should be amended to match once Jeremy agrees.

## What the playtesters said

- "Your first 20 minutes plan is too much... I remember seeing or encountering pretty much everything in that list. But I didn't hang onto any of it. Perhaps... make it over the first 24 hours. And expand upon each item in turn til we learn the world."
- "Your story box takes us immediately into interactions with the world outside of the tinker's shop, and we aren't quite ready for other characters and problems yet because we aren't grounded in the basics enough yet. I think that's why I had to read each of them about three times... all while I'm still trying to figure out what the mechanics of the game are."
- "I do need a reason to care about building wrenches and fighting rats." The early scenes "must either be high stakes choices (danger to the character to make them invested in the tinker as a person), or... something that allows the player to create something of their choice so they feel like your game is 'their' world." They "also all need to teach us who the tinker is, how we got here, why on earth rats are the enemy."
- "List out what your player needs to learn in order for mechanics and in a column next to it put the story points in order and don't make the player do too many steps on one screen. Plan out their learning progression."
- Earlier, from the same family: too many names too fast (work order 036), no hook (work order 035), and terms like Crowns, the Counting-House and "spike the bill" unexplained.

## What the first sitting asks of a player today

Measured by the Act I walk (work order 036): the first sitting runs about 29 minutes and ends with every Prologue scene played on exactly one full bar of Nerve. In that time a player meets:

- **Mechanics (12):** the Boiler and steam, queueing a job, waiting on a job, the Story tab and letters, Nerve, equipping, fighting, Rations, Crowns, selling at the Counting-House, paying a debt, Arrears. The guide then adds armour (Tailoring or Smithing) and the Loadout, and the scenes add challenges with odds.
- **Story:** ten scenes in six places (the workshop, the Row, the Polytechnic, Rustmoor's gate, a rat nest, a lecture hall), six named people or groups, and a dozen glossed terms.
- **And what it never says:** who you are, or why Hester's workshop came to you.

That is roughly one new thing a minute. Each piece is now explained (work orders 035 and 036), but explained is not learned: a player who is still finding the Start button cannot also hold a city's history. The fix is not better sentences. It is fewer things at once, in an order where each one is needed the moment it is taught.

## The rules this plan follows

1. **One new thing per screen.** A guide step teaches one control. A story scene introduces at most one new person, place or term, and only one that the scene needs.
2. **Teach a mechanic when the story needs it, and give a story reason for every mechanic.** You fight rats because they are in your workshop taking your spanners, not because the guide says so.
3. **The first sitting stays inside No. 9.** The street, the city and its institutions wait until the player can work the bench without thinking.
4. **The first scene is about you.** Who you are, why you are here, and one choice that is yours.
5. **Something is at stake early.** A cold Boiler that may not take the pressure; rats in the workshop at night. Danger to the Tinker, not to the city.
6. **Stories fill the waits.** A scene opens while a job runs, so reading never stops the work and the work never makes the reader wait. (The first playtester liked this most.)
7. **Leave a question at the end of every sitting,** answered at the start of the next.
8. **Spread it over the first day.** Three sittings: the first about 10 minutes, then two more a few hours apart. A sitting is a counted check-in (`record.check_ins`, an hour or more after the last).

## The progression

"You learn" is the one mechanic the step teaches. "You do" is the one thing the screen asks. "You can now say" is what a player should be able to tell someone afterwards; the educator checks these.

### Sitting 1: the workshop (about 10 minutes, all inside No. 9)

| # | You learn | You do | Story | You can now say |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Nothing yet | Read one short letter, sign in | The solicitor's letter: No. 9 Sprocket Row, "contents included", is yours. | "I've inherited a workshop in a city in the sky." |
| 2 | **The Boiler**: it runs your workshop for 12 hours, even while you are away | Open the valve | Brassbuckle, the professor with the keys, peels the Board's seal off a Boiler that has been cold since its last tenant left. Will it hold? It shudders, then holds. | "The Boiler keeps my workshop going while I'm gone." |
| 3 | **A job**: pick a thing to make, press Start, it takes time | Start a Brass Spanner | "Fifty Brass Ingots from the old stock." | "I pick what to make and it gets made over time." |
| 4 | **The Story tab**: scenes are choices; each costs Nerve, which refills | Play scene 1 while the spanner runs | **Who you are.** Why this workshop came to you, asked as a choice (see Decision 1). You find Hester's notebook binding with every page torn out. | "Hester Vane had this place before me and vanished. Her notebook's pages are gone." |
| 5 | **Stores**: what you make lands here | Watch the first spanner land | A first spanner, the first thing made at No. 9 since Hester left. | "My things go into my stores." |
| 6 | (story only) | Play scene 2 | **Rats in the workshop.** In the noise of the bench you hear scratching under the floor. Rust Rats, which come up the chains from the city's tip at night and take metal, have found your spanners. One bites. Choice: grab the spanner, or barricade and save the rest. | "Rats come for metal, and they're coming for mine." |
| 7 | **Equip and fight**: a fight runs on its own; you win or lose; Rations heal | Equip the spanner, fight a Rust Rat Runt | Brassbuckle: they come up from Rustmoor; go and make them think twice. | "My Tinker fights by itself with what I give it." |
| 8 | **Coming back**: the work goes on while you are away | Queue the night's work, press Leave it running | Brassbuckle leaves you the keys. As you lock up, the Pneumatic Post's tube rattles: something is on its way. | "I come back later and there's more." |

Four mechanics (Boiler, a job, the Story tab, a fight) and two scenes. The player leaves knowing who they are, what the rats want, and that Hester's pages are missing.

## What it changes

- **The guide** (`content/guide/first_session.json`, `Guide.tsx`): sitting 1 keeps the Boiler, the first job, the first scene, equip, fight and come back. The bill, selling and armour steps move to sitting 2 as goal lines, not guide steps. The guide's "Play a scene under Open to you" step that drives a player through every scene goes.
- **The Prologue**: scenes 1 and 2 are new or reworked so the first sitting stays inside No. 9 (who you are and the binding; rats in the workshop). The bill moves to sitting 2. The other scenes keep their order but are gated by `record.check_ins` so each sitting opens only its own. Ten scenes still fit the first day's Nerve (10 at the start, refilling 1 every 36 minutes).
- **Act I's first scene** opens at the sitting after the brass plate, as now.
- **GDD section 7**: "The first session (about 20 minutes)" becomes "The first day (three sittings)".

## Decisions for Jeremy

1. **Why did No. 9 come to you?** Canon does not say (characters.md lists "what Pip calls the player" as open). The playtester's best idea was a choice that makes the world yours. One scene could ask it: Hester's apprentice once; a distant relative named in her will; a stranger whose name she put down for reasons nobody knows. Each sets a flag that later lines can colour. If the answer belongs to a locked layer, say so and the scene asks something else that is yours (what you name the workshop, which of Hester's tools you keep).
2. **Looks.** Changing the Tinker's hair or outfit is a real hook in other games, but every pose and every weapon is drawn on one Tinker (four Gemini sheets today, with more as weapons are added), so each new look means redrawing all of them. Recommend: not now; revisit with Cog-bought looks (GDD section 10).
3. **Three sittings in the first day**, with the brass plate at the end of the third. Agreed (Jeremy, 3 November 2026).

## Measuring it

The educator (`.claude/agents/educator.md`) checks a draft of any of this against the "You can now say" column: it walks the first day in order, counts what each screen asks of a player, and runs cold reads with a reader who has never played an idle game. It tells the scrivener what was missed, confused, or dull, and the scrivener rewrites until the reader can say each line.

## Ledger

Kept by the educator: each concept, the step that teaches it, and the first step that needs it. Empty until its first run.
