## The city

Kettleport is a city of brick, brass and short tempers, riding a column of steam about a mile above the Scald, a white sea of boiling vapour that covers the world. Nobody alive has seen the ground. The city has been climbing for 312 years.

Stacked like an upturned wedding cake: Gaugehill on top, the Kettle-heart burning at the core, the working districts ringing the middle, the Lowvents hanging off the bottom rim close enough to the Scald that the windows sweat.

## The official story

In the Year of the Lift, the sea around the harbour town of Kettle's Reach began to boil. Seven engineers, remembered as the Seven Gauges, sank an iron shaft called the Downpipe into the seabed, built the Great Kettle on top of it, and rode the rising steam into the sky with the town bolted to their boilers. Their heirs sit on the Board of Gauges and read the city's great pressure gauges each morning to decide how high Kettleport flies.

The Brass Gazette prints the day's Gauge Reading on its front page. It is always reassuring.

## What everyone knows and nobody says

- **The Scald is rising.** Every year the vapour climbs, and every year the city climbs to stay ahead of it. Old Stokers swear it used to sit further down, and used to be quieter.
- **Ballast Day.** When the Kettle can lift no more, the Board empties a district, unbolts it, and drops it into the Scald so the rest can rise. The last one was 41 years ago. The Lowvents have never been ballast, and everyone there knows which way gravity works.
- **Steam is rationed.** Every workshop draws steam from the Kettle's mains through a metered Boiler, and the Board licenses the hours.
- **The dead go up the flue.** There is no ground to bury anyone in. The dead are given to the Cinderworks furnaces with a short service and a long whistle. Asking about the soot is rude.

## Daily life

Runs on tea; water is the one thing the city has too much of, every corner has a public urn, and a good Tea Blender is treated like a minor saint. Time is kept by the Great Whistle, which sounds the hours from the Kettle-heart. Nobody admits that it sometimes sounds a note that is not on the hour.

Money is the Crown, a heavy brass coin stamped with a gauge needle. Gilded Cogs are Counting-House certificates that most citizens hold and few understand. Fog rises from the Scald; storms roll down from the Thunderhead.

## District detail

Canonised by Jeremy on 28 October 2026, from work order 023's scenes.

- **Sprocket Row** brings its broken kettles and clocks to a Tinker's step, and pays in what it has, wrapped in the Gazette.
- **Pip Tumbler** carries messages along the Row for Rations.
- **Rustmoor:** Gaugehill's dust carts empty at the tip gate at dusk, and the pickers call the new pile the fresh heap. Gaugehill throws out its coal half-burnt in the ash sacks. The pickers keep a common supper pot in the lee of the crane.
- **The Lowvents:** the Fellowship hall's stove bench is won by arm-wrestling. Somebody reads the Gazette's Gauge Reading aloud every evening, and nobody looks up. Mother Ash keeps a slate of split pipes by the hall door, which anyone may chalk; she reads all of it, including who wrote it. The stair pays a Tinker in coal.
- **The Polytechnic:** Brassbuckle's first-years build boilers on a practical bench in the basement, and a qualified hand must test each before it is lit.
- **The Brass Exchange** closes at a bell. Paid runners carry the day's trade slips to the Counting-House vault, and the House sets their running against what they owe it; a chief clerk runs them, and red ink in his book is good news. The price board is a board of brass flaps. A public gallery with a padded bench is open to anyone.
